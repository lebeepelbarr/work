Deal of the Week

From Facebook lead to wholetail

Rickey offered $15K less than the other guy but still got the deal. Here’s how!

Rickey Miller wasn’t expecting a massive rehab when he walked into this Killeen, Texas, property. The four-bedroom house was in great shape, in a desirable neighborhood, and needed almost no renovation.

He saw a chance to buy, clean it up, and relist. Then came the competition. Other investors were offering roughly $15,000 more.

The Killeen property behind Rickey’s wholetail deal.

How did the lower offer win?

It started with a Facebook inquiry. Rickey contacted the seller that day and walked the property the next.

Once he understood the seller’s relocation plans, he discussed an offer and put it in writing.

But the seller had options. Better-paying ones, too.

Rickey kept the conversation going rather than assuming another investor would win on price. He credits the rapport they built for the seller’s decision to move forward with him.

After the agreement, the team still had to manage the communication and paperwork involved in closing. The acquisition came together, and Rickey pursued a light-cleanup wholetail strategy. He reports $60,279 in profit, with renovation costs under $2,000.

The lesson

“Never pre-judge a lead based on the property’s condition or the seller’s initial asking price.”

— Rickey Miller

A clean house isn’t necessarily a dead lead. And getting an offer accepted isn’t always about adding another $15,000.
