How Chris Kept a $30,160 Deal Alive for 145 Days

An inherited 3-bed, 2-bath in Florence, South Carolina, with no rehab planned. The numbers worked on day one. Getting it closed took 145 days.

The story

One Inherited House. One Hundred Forty-Five Days.

Here’s how the deal actually played out.

  1. A Clean Spread With No Rehab

    Chris secured the property for $160,000 against a reported value of $202,000. With no renovation planned, he could avoid construction costs and pursue a retail buyer through a novation. On paper, it was the kind of setup investors want: a simple property, a clear exit, and room in the numbers.

  2. The Seller Became the Bottleneck

    Once the agreement was signed, the seller became difficult to reach and didn’t consistently make the house available for showings. That put the deal at risk. The buyer, appraiser, and inspectors still needed access. Every unanswered call or missed appointment created another reason for the buyer to lose patience and walk away.

  3. The Delays Stacked Up

    The appraisal had to be rescheduled. The buyer requested additional inspection work. Then came a plumbing issue, a $750 buyer credit, moving expenses, lender requirements, and closing logistics that kept shifting. None of those issues was impossible to solve. Together, they created a long list of details that needed an owner, a deadline, and a documented next step.

  4. Follow-Up Became the Strategy

    Chris’s team kept calling, texting, documenting, and following up. Not for a week, but for as long as the transaction required. That consistency protected the buyer relationship, kept open items from disappearing, and gave the team a way to move the deal forward even when the seller went quiet again.

  5. Clear to Close

    The seller signed the final papers, and the property was marked sold. Chris reported a $30,160 profit with no rehab dollars invested. The problems didn’t suddenly disappear. The team simply kept working the next problem until there were none left.

“Make sure you’re extra clear in the beginning, once a contract is signed, that calls need to be answered and sellers need to be responsive.” — Chris Gormley

Exit strategy

Why Novation Fit

The property didn’t need a renovation project to appeal to a retail buyer. A novation allowed Chris to work toward that retail sale without funding a rehab or managing contractors.

The tradeoff was dependence on seller cooperation. Showings, inspections, the appraisal, documents, and final signatures still had to happen. Novation reduced the rehab risk; it didn’t remove the transaction risk.

The real reason

Why This Deal Actually Closed

A deal doesn’t survive 145 days on luck. Chris’s team carried this one to the table with three habits:

Relentless follow-up Visible ownership Clear expectations

Relentless follow-up: Silence triggered another next step, not an assumption that the deal was dead.

Visible ownership: Each appraisal, inspection, plumbing, lender, and closing item had someone responsible for moving it.

Clear expectations: The seller was reminded that signing the contract didn’t end their role in the transaction.

Take it to your next deal

What to Take Into Your Next Deal

  • 01
    Set Communication Expectations at Signing
    Explain that the seller will still need to respond after the contract is signed. Showings, inspections, appraisals, document requests, and closing all require participation.
  • 02
    Build a Backup Access Plan
    Decide early who can open the property if the seller is unavailable. A relative, neighbor, agent, or lockbox can keep one missed call from becoming a missed appointment.
  • 03
    Track the Transaction, Not Just the Contract
    “Under contract” feels like a win, but the spread pays only when the deal closes. Make the current blocker, its owner, and the next follow-up date obvious to everyone on the team.
“Signing gets us started, but we’ll still need your help with access, documents, and closing. What’s the fastest way to reach you? And who can help us if you’re unavailable?”
