Deal of the Week · A Real Deal, Broken Down

He almost lost $30,160 because the seller stopped answering

Have you ever had a deal that looked clean right up until the contract was signed?

Chris Gormley had one in Florence, South Carolina: an inherited 3-bed, 2-bath with no rehab planned and roughly $42,000 between the contract price and the reported value.

The deal looked clean. Then the seller became difficult to reach.

Here’s Where It Got Complicated

Access for showings kept falling through. The appraisal had to be rescheduled, the buyer requested more inspection work, and a plumbing issue added another delay. By that point, a $750 buyer credit, moving expenses, lender conditions, and shifting closing details had turned a clean deal into a long list of unresolved items.

At that point, Chris could either assume the deal was gone or spend time and resources chasing down the lead.

He and his team chose the latter. One call, one task, and one follow-up at a time. Until the deal closed on Day 145.

The Lesson Chris Took From It

“Make sure you’re extra clear in the beginning, once a contract is signed, that calls need to be answered and sellers need to be responsive.”

— Chris Gormley

The contract starts the transaction. It doesn’t end the seller’s involvement. Before your next signing, ask:

“What’s the fastest way to reach you, and who can help us get access if you’re unavailable?”

That one conversation can prevent weeks of uncertainty later.
