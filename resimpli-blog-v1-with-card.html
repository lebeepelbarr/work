REsimpli is proprietary software built for real estate investors: one system that takes a deal from the list to the closing table to the P&L. Left Main REI is a real estate investor CRM built on Salesforce. Both are real products for the right buyer. This article is about which buyer you are.

Everything here about Left Main REI comes from its published pricing, Terms of Service, and knowledge base as of October 19, 2026, and links to the clause or page it comes from. Everything about REsimpli reflects the latest version, released on September 12, 2026.

1. Why we built REsimpli

Before REsimpli, I closed more than 500 real estate transactions. The hard part of that business was never finding deals. It was running it across six pieces of software that did not know about each other: a CRM here, a dialer there, a separate texting platform, a disconnected skip-tracing tool, and numbers on QuickBooks.

REsimpli exists so an investor can go from a list to a closed deal and a P&L, and change how that system works without filing a ticket. The latest version of REsimpli, 6.0, is the biggest step we have taken toward that: a rebuild of pipelines, automations, dialing, dashboards, and bookkeeping around how an investing company actually operates.

2. Stay on Left Main if

  • You have full-time Salesforce developers on staff
  • You’d rather pay for the CRM, then pay again for phones, texting, a dialer, a website, buyer data, and maps, and manage every vendor yourself.
  • You have a process you won’t change often, and you have a budget for Professional Services hours when you do.
  • You want lending and title referrals inside the CRM. Left Main introduces you to third-party partners. REsimpli doesn’t.

Left Main’s own comparison, REsimpli Alternatives for Growing Real Estate Teams, put the difference this way: REsimpli is built to replace apps, while Left Main is built to build companies. We’d put it differently. Left Main is built to extend Salesforce. REsimpli is built to run an investing business.

If the bullets above describe you, stay on Left Main. If they don’t, read on.

3. Why growing teams leave Left Main REI

Investors who move from Left Main to REsimpli tend to name the same reasons. Each one traces back to something in Left Main’s own pricing, contract, or documentation.

  • They were hiring the wrong team. This is the one we hear most. An investor signs up to grow an acquisitions team and ends up growing a Salesforce team instead: an administrator to make the changes, a consultant for the ones the administrator can’t, and a standing budget of Professional Services hours. Left Main’s pricing page lists “Customization” and “Cadence Configuration” as Enterprise features; its knowledge base is written for Salesforce administrators and sends non-trivial questions to Salesforce itself. Every hour spent in Object Manager is an hour not spent on the phone with a seller. On REsimpli, the person who changes the pipeline is the person who runs it.
  • The bill kept growing. The CRM was the smallest line. Phones, the dialer, texting, the website, buyer data, Salesforce Maps at $45 a user, and county subscriptions for property data each arrived on a separate invoice. The five-person example in section 3 lands at $1,469 a month against $699, before the $3,000 implementation fee.
  • Every change waited on someone else. A new lead status is an 18-step walkthrough in Salesforce Object Manager. Cadence automations ship disabled until an administrator configures them. Retired components are removed by the customer. Once the included Professional Services hours are spent, the next change is a quote.
  • They hit a ceiling. Enterprise includes 10 seats, with extra users at $80 a month; seats can be added any time but reduced only at the anniversary (Terms, 2.4). And every customer warrants it has 75 or fewer employees, with breach and termination above that (Terms, 3.4).
  • The data was a week old and metered. Property Sales AI refreshes every 7 days, matches at 99%, and recommends a person review the misses. DispoSignals allows 20 buyer searches a month. County subscriptions cannot be swapped once bought. REsimpli refreshes daily, nationwide, with unlimited buyer searches.
  • Salesforce got in the way. A user who logs in over a VPN can be frozen by Salesforce before anyone reviews it. A Salesforce security update broke buttons in August 2026. Salesforce sets the MFA and login rules, and Left Main’s support excludes Salesforce platform issues (Exhibit B).
  • Help came by ticket. Support is Monday to Friday, 8am to 5pm Eastern, with a 24-hour response target, and getting help on your org means granting Left Main login access for a month. Integrations and custom development are Professional Services at the client’s cost.
  • Leaving cost as much as staying. The full annual term is owed if you stop, and on termination access is suspended and data terminated while charges continue (Terms, 2.1 and Exhibit A). Notes cannot be exported without SOQL queries or paid services.

None of these is a complaint about Left Main’s intent. They are what “built on Salesforce” costs a customer in practice: money, time, and headcount that should have gone to the real estate business, spent on the platform underneath it instead. They are also the reasons section 9 exists.

5. Getting set up, and getting help

On REsimpli, you can start using the platform the same day. On Left Main REI, it is a project with a fee and a budget of hours.

REsimpli. Import your leads and contacts, pick up phone numbers, build your pipelines and automations in settings, and run the dialer that afternoon. Onboarding is unlimited on every plan: an account manager is assigned at signup, setup calls are unlimited, and support runs on live Zoom, not a ticket queue.

Left Main REI. Starter is $500 a month plus a $3,000 implementation fee. Launch folds implementation in and adds 5 Professional Services hours, while Enterprise adds “premium implementation” and 60 hours.

Read those numbers as Left Main REI telling you how much setup there is. Software that is ready to use on day one does not need a $3,000 fee or a 60-hour budget to configure it.

Left Main’s own docs say its implementation team connects new customers’ phone and SMS providers, that cadence automations ship disabled until an administrator configures them, and that existing customers adopting Next Best Action face a 219-step post-installation guide.

Support afterward is by ticket, Monday to Friday 8am to 5pm Eastern, with a 24-hour response target, Salesforce platform issues excluded (Terms, Exhibit B).

How long it takes. Left Main’s features page says most investors are up and running within 24 to 48 hours. That is a login. A configured system, with pipelines mapped to your process, cadences built and switched on, a phone provider connected, data migrated, overlapping Salesforce Flows cleared, and retired components removed, is what the 60 hours are for. Full implementation on Left Main REI can take 90 days or more.

6. Would you buy a car without driving it?

Nobody buys a car off a brochure. You sit in it, you drive it, you find out whether it fits before you sign anything. A CRM is the car your whole team drives every day for a year. The same rule should apply.

How Left Main sells. Every tier on the pricing page ends in “Talk to sales.” There is a guided demo on their site. Then a sales call. Then an annual agreement, billed monthly, with the full term owed if you stop (Terms, Section 2.1), and access suspended and your data terminated on termination while the charges continue (Exhibit A). You watch the salesperson drive it around the lot, and then you sign a twelve-month lease.

That is not a policy choice. It is what the product requires. Software that needs a $3,000 implementation and a 60-hour services budget cannot hand you a login on day one, because there is nothing to drive until it is configured. No trial is the honest consequence of a product that has to be configured before it works.

How REsimpli sells. Start a 14-day trial, today, without talking to anyone. Import your own leads. Pick up a phone number and make real calls on the triple-line dialer. Build a pipeline the way your team works. Send a text, book an appointment, watch it land on the dashboard. At the end of two weeks, upgrade or walk away, owing nothing.

A demo shows you what the software can do. A trial shows you what your team will do with it at four o’clock on a Tuesday with forty leads in the queue. Whether the calls connect, whether the acquisitions manager opens it without being told to, whether the numbers on the dashboard are ones you’d run the business on. That is what you are buying, and it is the one thing a sales call cannot show you.

Take the car home for two weeks. If it fits, keep it.

7. Built for real estate investors

REsimpli is a dedicated CRM for real estate investors, built and run by real estate investors, and the feature stack proves why that matters.

  • Automation triggers that only matter to an investor: direct mail delivered or returned, appointment kept or not kept, offer amount above or below a threshold, contract price versus estimated value.
  • A triple-line dialer in every module, for every user, with call outcomes that trigger automations.
  • A buyer-facing dispo site with reserve pricing, Buy Now, offer deadlines, and offer submission with proof of funds.
  • Comps AI that reads the seller conversation to estimate condition, then produces ARV, repairs, and MAO (AI add-on).
  • Dashboards built around the investor funnel (lead, qualified, appointment, offer, contract, sold) with speed-to-lead, contract fallout, and a 30-day cash-flow forecast.
  • List stacking with property data refreshed daily, and a Daily Update dashboard that flags new pre-foreclosures, recent sales, MLS changes, and vacancy changes inside your own database.
  • Driving for Dollars with routed drives, geofenced actions, and driver-only seats that do not count as users.
  • Bookkeeping by property and scope of work, so a flip’s budget-to-actual turns red inside the deal.

None of these are integrations. They are one system, designed around the deal, maintained by one company that answers to investors and no one else.

8. REsimpli is built to scale

REsimpli is built for the whole range, from a first deal to multiple markets with dedicated acquisition, disposition, and operations teams. 6.0 added what larger teams asked for: up to 20 pipelines with 20 stages each in every module, lead access scoped by campaign, pipeline, or assignment, multiple acquisition and lead managers on one lead, unlimited custom roles, enforced two-factor authentication with device login history, multi-entity books, an Open API, and a Business plan with 15 users that can be expanded. Many REsimpli customers close 100+ deals a year on it today.

One contract note for anyone planning to grow: Left Main’s Terms of Service (Section 3.4) require every customer to warrant it has 75 or fewer employees, and treat exceeding that as a breach that permits termination. REsimpli’s terms have no employee cap.

9. Built on Salesforce vs. built for investors

When you buy REsimpli, you get one piece of software, written by one company, for one kind of customer. The data model is a deal: lead, contact, property, buyer, transaction, inventory, rental. The dialer, the automations, the dashboards, the dispo site, and the books are all parts of that one system.

When you buy Left Main REI, you get a Salesforce org with Left Main’s investor objects, cadences, and data products installed in it. That is their description, not ours: their site says the platform is built on Salesforce and carries a Salesforce Consulting Partner badge in its footer. Everything that makes it a CRM (the database, the interface, the reporting engine, the automation tooling, the security, the hosting, the release cycle) is Salesforce’s.

The practical difference is who can change things and how fast. On REsimpli, pipelines, stages, fields, roles, call outcomes, automations, dashboards, and notification rules are settings you change yourself, today.

On Left Main REI, changes go through Salesforce configuration; the person doing that work needs to know Salesforce, not real estate, and anything beyond the included hours is Professional Services at the client’s cost. And because Salesforce sets its own release schedule, Left Main REI has to absorb platform changes before it builds anything for you.

REsimpli 6.0 shipped 20-stage pipelines, a triple-line dialer, native automations, and a bookkeeping engine because investors asked for them, not because a platform permitted them.

Built on Salesforce means, in practice, built for the people who know Salesforce. REsimpli is built for the people who close deals.

From the documentation

The knowledge base is written for Salesforce administrators, and it reads like it. Six things a buyer learns there:

  • Changing a lead status is an 18-step walkthrough in Salesforce Object Manager, across the picklist editor and a field-dependency matrix (Add a New Lead Status). The “Customize Left Main” section of the docs contains two articles. On REsimpli, a stage is a drag in pipeline settings.
  • Property Sales AI refreshes every 7 days, matches addresses at 99% accuracy, plans no enhancements for the misses, and recommends a person review matches weekly or monthly (data guide). The desk reference still calls it a beta. REsimpli refreshes daily.
  • The QuickBooks integration is one-way into one marketing dashboard and is explicitly not for invoicing, taxes, or CPA reporting (overview). It is also Enterprise-only.
  • Mapping is Salesforce Maps at $45 per user per month, installed by your Salesforce administrator (purchasing Maps).
  • Salesforce can freeze a user for logging in over a VPN, before any admin reviews it, and the user is locked out of Left Main until your administrator restores the account (account frozen). If you run offshore cold callers or VAs, read that page twice.
  • Notes cannot be exported with a report or object export; getting them out means SOQL queries and a spreadsheet join, or paid services (notes export). Price that in before you ever want to leave.

The pattern across Left Main’s documentation: when something needs doing, the answer is your Salesforce administrator, Left Main Professional Services, or Salesforce itself. The pattern across REsimpli’s is a settings page.

Frequently asked questions

Is Left Main REI a white-label version of Salesforce?
No, and we are not saying it is. Left Main is built on Salesforce under a partner distribution agreement, with its own investor objects, cadences, and data products (DealSignals, DispoSignals, Property Sales AI) added on top. What you license is Left Main’s configuration of Salesforce, restricted by Salesforce’s embedded-edition terms (Terms of Service, Section 3.5). It is not Salesforce itself, and it is not a standalone CRM.
Does Left Main require a Salesforce developer?
Not for everything. Changes beyond the Professional Services hours included in your plan (none listed on Starter, 5 on Launch, 60 on Enterprise) are billable, and the person making them works in Salesforce configuration. On REsimpli, pipelines, stages, fields, roles, automations, and dashboards are settings you change yourself.
Does Left Main include calling, SMS, or a dialer?
Left Main’s documentation lists four phone providers that work with its cadences (CallRail, Dialpad, RingCentral, smrtPhone), each through its own package install and subscription, and describes no native calling. REsimpli includes unlimited calling and SMS and a triple-line dialer for every user on every plan.
What does Left Main cost for a five-person team?
Starter is $500 a month plus $3,000 implementation. With phones, a multi-line dialer, a website, and eSign from vendors on Left Main’s integration wall, the worked example above comes to about $1,469 a month and $20,628 in year one, against $699 a month and $8,388 for REsimpli Team.
Does REsimpli have an equivalent to DealSignals and DispoSignals?
In REsimpli, Daily Update and list stacking cover motivation changes in your database. Lost Deal covers what Property Sales AI reports. Cash buyer search and comps cover DispoSignals. They are not identical, but they are included, with no county subscription and no 20-search cap.
Does REsimpli work for large teams?
Yes. 6.0 was rebuilt for it: 20-pipeline modules, scoped access, custom roles, enforced 2FA, multi-entity books, and a Business plan with 15 users that can be expanded. Customers close 100+ deals a year on it. Left Main’s terms cap customers at 75 employees; REsimpli’s do not.

The short version

REsimpli is proprietary software built for real estate investors, with the tools an investor runs the business on included in the subscription.

Left Main REI is a Salesforce org with an investor layer on top, and the implementation fee, metered customization, vendor stack, county-priced data, and 75-employee contract follow from that. If your company already runs on Salesforce, Left Main REI may be the right trade. If it doesn’t, you would be buying a platform to get a product. REsimpli is the product.
