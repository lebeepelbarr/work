A Real Deal, Broken Down

A lesson bigger than the $70K wholesale fee

Cameron made $70,000 on this wholesale. But the takeaway isn't the assignment fee, or the direct mail campaign that landed the deal. It is the communication mistake that almost complicated the entire transaction.

The Sanford, FL package deal · 5 houses bought at $600K, wholesaled for a $70K fee

A landlord in Sanford, FL wanted to sell five rental properties as one package. Cameron Moore locked them up for $600,000, assigned the contract, and closed 45 days later with a $70,000 assignment fee.

On paper, it looks straightforward.

In reality, five occupied properties meant five sets of tenants, inspections, walkthroughs, documents, and moving parts.

The biggest lesson? Never outsource communication that affects your closing. Cameron's team trusted the seller to notify tenants about access, which didn't happen. That one decision created unnecessary friction throughout the transaction.

Three takeaways worth stealing

1. Portfolio deals should be underwritten as one investment. Looking at five properties individually hides the opportunity. Looking at the combined spread revealed plenty of room for both Cameron and the end buyer.
2. Own every important conversation. If inspections, walkthroughs, or tenant access affect your timeline, your team, not the seller, should control that communication.
3. Complexity compounds fast. One property is manageable. Five properties create dozens of conversations, files, buyers, and deadlines that all have to stay synchronized. That's where systems matter.
