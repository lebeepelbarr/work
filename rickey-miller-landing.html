Deal summary

A good house, a motivated seller, but Rickey couldn’t win on the price

The property was in an excellent neighborhood and needed very little work. Its owner was relocating, and Rickey saw an opportunity to purchase the house, clean it up, and put it back on the market.

The challenge wasn’t the condition of the house. It was the competition. Other investors were offering substantially more, leaving Rickey to compete on something besides price.

How Rickey won the deal

  1. A Facebook lead worth calling

    The lead arrived through a Facebook ad. Rickey was local, so he reached out the same day the seller submitted the form.

    The property wasn’t a rundown house waiting for a major rehab. It was a relatively modern home in a desirable neighborhood, with little obvious work needed.

    That could have been a reason to move on. Instead, Rickey picked up the phone, learned about the relocation, and scheduled a walkthrough for the following day.

  2. One walkthrough showed the opportunity

    At the appointment, Rickey got a closer look at the four-bedroom, three-bathroom house. The condition supported a much simpler plan than a traditional fix-and-flip.

    Rickey saw a possible wholetail: buy the property, do a quick cleanup, and relist it.

    He discussed an offer with the seller over the phone and followed up in writing. The numbers were worth pursuing. Getting the seller to agree would be another matter.

  3. The competing offers came in higher

    Rickey wasn’t the only investor interested. According to him, the seller received other offers roughly $15,000 higher than his. On price alone, those offers had the advantage.

    But Rickey had already spent time talking with the seller, learning about the move, and explaining his offer. Rather than treating it as a race to the highest number, he focused on building a relationship the seller felt comfortable with.

    That approach paid off. The deal went under contract at $225,000.

  4. From accepted offer to closing

    Getting the contract signed was an important step, but there was still a transaction to complete.

    Communication continued as the deal moved through the closing process. Agreements, appointments, documents, and status updates all needed attention.

    The investor reports 19 days from lead to close.

  5. A wholetail instead of a major renovation

    Rickey kept the work light. The renovation cost came in just under $2,000.

    The result wasn’t a dramatic property transformation. It was recognizing that a well-maintained house in a good market could produce a strong return with minimal intervention.

Exit strategy

Why wholetailing made sense

A full renovation can make sense when a property needs substantial work to become marketable. That wasn’t the situation here. Rickey was dealing with a 2003-built home in an excellent neighborhood that already presented well.

The opportunity was to buy at a workable number, make minor improvements, and return the property to the market. A wholetail fit that condition and his goal of maximizing returns without spending unnecessarily on renovations.

The real reason

Why Rickey got the deal

Three decisions stand out.

  • 1
    He contacted the seller immediately. Rickey didn’t dismiss the lead because the house looked too nice or because the owner might have other selling options.
  • 2
    He invested in the conversation. The seller received higher offers, but Rickey credits the relationship they developed for helping him win the contract.
  • 3
    He followed through after the offer. Communication, documentation, and coordination continued from the initial appointment through closing.

Take it to your next deal

What another investor can learn

  • •
    Call the lead before deciding it isn’t a deal. A property’s condition or asking price doesn’t tell you everything about the owner’s priorities. Have the conversation first.
  • •
    Don’t assume the highest offer always wins. Price matters, but so do communication, expectations, and the seller’s confidence in the buyer.
  • •
    Match the exit to the property. If a house needs very little work, don’t automatically build a full renovation into your plan. Evaluate whether a lighter strategy makes more sense.
  • •
    Keep the next step clear after getting a contract. An accepted offer still requires coordination. Make sure communication, documents, and outstanding actions are easy to find.
